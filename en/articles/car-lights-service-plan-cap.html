Headlight and mirror service plan: what happens when the part costs more than the cap

  • Court: Safed Small Claims Court
  • Case: Small Claim 9667-02-26
  • Decided 27.10.2026
  • Published: November 2, 2026

A driver paid monthly for a headlight and mirror replacement plan and was asked to pay ILS 804 for a new headlight. She called the charge excessive. The court found it matched the coverage document she had attached herself.

What happened

On top of her car insurance, the claimant had a monthly service plan for repairing and replacing headlights and mirrors. In June 2024 her front headlight was damaged. The company asked her to pay ILS 804 for the replacement. She refused: in her view the plan set a deductible of only ILS 250, and she had found a new original headlight for about ILS 2,150. She had the car repaired herself and sued for ILS 4,840 for the repair, expenses and distress.

What the plan said

Under the terms, the subscriber pays a ILS 250 deductible, and the company gets quotes from the parts importers it works with. If the part plus installation costs less than ILS 4,000, nothing more is due. Above that, the subscriber pays the difference over ILS 4,000. According to the company, a new original headlight cost ILS 4,695.

The decision

The senior registrar found that the claimant had misread the charge. It was not an "excessive deductible" but three separate items: the deductible, an installation fee and the amount above the ILS 4,000 cap. These matched the coverage document the claimant herself attached.

The court also accepted the company's explanation of its practice of working with fixed suppliers. A company serving many subscribers need not hunt for the cheapest part and garage in every case. Finding a lower price elsewhere does not prove the company acted unreasonably.

The claim was dismissed. The court did not, however, order the claimant to pay costs, given her many calls to the service center and the incomplete answers she received for too long.

Key takeaways

  • In any service plan or policy, look for three numbers: the deductible, the coverage cap and what happens above it.
  • Before repairing on your own, ask the company for a written, itemized breakdown of what it is charging.
  • A cheaper price elsewhere is not enough to win. You need to show the company departed from the contract or acted unreasonably.
  • Keep a record of every call. Even when a claim fails, poor service can affect costs.
